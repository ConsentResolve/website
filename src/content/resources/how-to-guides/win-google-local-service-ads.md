## Introduction

Google Local Service Ads sit at the very top of the search results — above the Map Pack, above the regular ads — wearing a green Google Guaranteed badge that homeowners trust on sight. Better still, you pay per lead rather than per click. That sounds like a dream, and it can be. But left unmanaged, LSA quietly drains money on leads that were never going to become customers: wrong-number calls, out-of-area requests, services you do not offer. The contractors who win at LSA are not the ones who spend the most. They are the ones who set it up tightly and dispute disciplined. This guide shows you how.

## Who This Is For

Home-service businesses that are licensed and insured, can pass a background check, and want high-intent leads from the top of Google. If you are already running LSA but are not sure whether it is profitable — or you are paying for leads you never review — this will tighten the operation.

## Why It Matters

The top of the search page is the most valuable real estate that exists for a local business, and the Google Guaranteed badge converts because it removes the homeowner's biggest fear: hiring a stranger. Pay-per-lead also means your spend maps directly to opportunities, not clicks. But the same model punishes inattention — every junk lead you fail to dispute is money you handed Google for nothing. Run LSA well and your cost per booked job can beat almost any other paid channel.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Pass the background and license check.** This is the gate. Submit your business license, insurance, and the required background checks promptly. You cannot earn the Guaranteed badge or run ads until you clear it, so start here.

2. **Set your budget by job value, not gut feel.** Calculate what a booked job is worth and what share of leads you typically close. Work backward to a cost-per-lead you can afford, then set your weekly budget accordingly. Do not guess a round number.

3. **Optimize the profile for the algorithm.** Fill out every service category you offer, set accurate service areas, list realistic hours, and keep your reviews flowing. LSA ranking is driven heavily by responsiveness, review volume and recency, proximity, and your booking rate — so completeness and activity matter.

4. **Respond inside the speed window.** LSA rewards fast responders and penalizes missed calls. Answer the phone or return the message immediately. Slow or missed responses lower your ranking and waste leads you already paid for.

5. **Dispute the right leads, with the right wording.** Review every lead. Dispute the ones that are genuinely invalid — outside your service area, for a service you do not provide, spam, or a duplicate. Be specific and factual in the dispute reason; vague disputes get denied. Disciplined disputing is the single biggest lever on your effective cost per lead.

6. **Stack LSA with your organic Map Pack.** LSA and your Google Business Profile reinforce each other. The reviews and responsiveness you build for one help the other. Run both rather than treating LSA as a substitute for organic local presence.

## Common Mistakes

The costliest mistake is never reviewing leads, so bad ones are never disputed and your real cost per lead balloons. Others: setting a budget with no connection to job economics, letting calls go to voicemail (which both loses the lead and lowers your rank), disputing everything indiscriminately until Google stops honoring your disputes, and abandoning organic local SEO because "LSA covers it." LSA is a complement, not a replacement.

## Compliance Considerations

LSA requires legitimate licensing and insurance — never misrepresent credentials. Dispute honestly; fraudulent disputes can get you removed from the program. And when an LSA lead does not book on the first call, follow up only through channels the prospect has consented to. The lead reached out to you, which is your basis for contact — but keep that follow-up clean and consent-respecting, exactly the standard Consent Resolve enforces, rather than resorting to scraped numbers or relentless cold dialing.
