## Definition

**Website visitor identification** is the practice of turning anonymous website visitors into known contacts a business can follow up with. The consent-first version only identifies visitors who have given clear, logged permission to be identified and contacted.

## Plain English Meaning

Most of the people who land on your website never fill out a form or call. Analytics counts them, but you never learn who they are, so you cannot follow up. Visitor identification closes that gap by matching a consenting visitor to a real contact — a name and an email you are allowed to use. The key word is *consenting*: the safe version asks first, the risky version does not.

## Example

A homeowner searches "water heater replacement near you," lands on a plumber's site, reads the pricing page, and leaves without calling. With analytics alone, that visit is a number on a chart. With consent-first visitor identification, if that homeowner accepted a clear consent banner, the plumber receives a real name and a consented email — so the plumber can send one helpful follow-up inviting the homeowner to call or finish a quote. The homeowner reaches out to the contractor, not the other way around.

## Why It Matters

Anonymous traffic is usually a contractor's single biggest leak — far bigger than form abandonment. Recovering even a slice of it adds leads at almost no extra cost, because you already paid to bring those people to the site. But *how* you recover them decides whether you have built a durable lead engine or a lawsuit. Scrape-and-cold-call tools expose you to per-violation penalties and torch trust. Consent-first identification captures the same upside while staying on the right side of the law and the customer relationship.
