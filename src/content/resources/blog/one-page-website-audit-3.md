## You don't need a $2,000 audit to find the leak

Every quarter or so a contractor gets an email offering a "free website audit" that turns into a $2,000 sales pitch. You don't need it. The leaks that actually cost you jobs are visible in about ten minutes if you know where to look — and you can look from your own phone, standing in the truck.

So this quarter, before you spend a dime on agency consulting or a new design, run the one-page audit below. It's built to surface your *biggest* leak fast, then fix that one thing rather than boiling the ocean.

## Why your site leaks even when it looks fine

Here's the backdrop that makes the audit matter. A site can look clean, load fine, and still hemorrhage business — because the problem usually isn't looks. The average visitor spends about [87 seconds](https://spectruminfinite.com/blogs/average-time-spent-on-website-2025/) on a site before moving on, and across home-service websites roughly [98% of visitors never convert or identify themselves](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/conversion-rate-benchmarks).

That means a pretty site that can't capture its visitors is still a leaky bucket. The audit isn't about design taste. It's about finding where the people you already paid to attract slip out the bottom.

## The 10-minute, one-page audit (print this)

Open your site on your phone — that's where most homeowners are — and walk these eight checks. Print this list, tape it to the wall, and run it every quarter.

- **[ ] Speed:** Does the homepage load in a couple of seconds, or do you stare at a blank screen? Slow loads lose people before the 87-second clock even starts.
- **[ ] The phone test:** In five seconds, can a stranger see what you do, where you do it, and your phone number — without scrolling? If not, the top of your page is wasting its best real estate.
- **[ ] One clear next step:** Is there a single obvious action above the fold ("Get a quote," "Call now"), or three competing buttons? Confused visitors pick nothing.
- **[ ] Proof, fast:** Are reviews, photos, or a recognizable name visible early? Trust signals have to land inside that first minute and a half.
- **[ ] Service area:** Can a visitor instantly confirm you cover their town? Doubt about coverage sends people back to search.
- **[ ] Mobile forms:** Is your contact form short and thumb-friendly, or a wall of fields nobody finishes on a phone?
- **[ ] The capture gap:** This is the big one. *Other than the form, do you have any way to know who visited and follow up?* For most contractors the honest answer is no.
- **[ ] Same-day follow-up:** When someone does reach out, can you respond within minutes — or does it sit until tomorrow?

Score it honestly. Whichever line gets the loudest "no" is where your money is leaking first.

## What's the one fix most audits point to?

Nearly every contractor who runs this lands on the same line: **the capture gap.** Speed and layout matter, but even a perfect page only works on the sliver of visitors willing to fill out a form. The other ~98% — the people who showed up, browsed, and left in under ninety seconds — vanish with no name, no email, no second chance.

Here's why fixing the capture gap pays back faster than anything else on the list. Speed and layout tweaks make a good page slightly better at converting the small slice of people already willing to act. Closing the capture gap unlocks the entire other side of the ledger — the people who were never going to fill out a form no matter how clean your page looked. You're not squeezing a few more percent out of the form-fillers; you're reaching the majority you've been losing completely. That's the difference between polishing the front window and turning on the lights inside.
