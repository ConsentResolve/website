## The call that looked like it came out of nowhere

A homeowner calls Thursday, ready to book a big job, and it feels like they found you that morning. They didn't. They'd been on your site twice before — once last week comparing you to two other shops, once the weekend before that just getting a feel for your prices. By the time they called, they'd already decided. You just never saw the first two visits.

That's how your best jobs actually arrive. Not from a single click and a form fill, but from a quiet stretch of research you mostly can't see.

It changes how you should think about your website traffic. The reports show you visits and bounces, and most of those bounces look like wasted clicks. But a chunk of them aren't people leaving forever — they're people leaving to think, and they'll be back. The visit that looks like a failure today is often step one of a job you book next week.

## Why the biggest jobs come slowest

The bigger the decision, the longer the runway. A homeowner about to spend real money doesn't commit on a first look — they research in passes. They check your reviews one day, compare prices another, sleep on it, come back. And each of those visits is short: the average person spends about [87 seconds](https://spectruminfinite.com/blogs/average-time-spent-on-website-2025/) on a site before moving on, which is plenty to form an impression and nowhere near enough to commit.

So the customer cycles through two or three visits — and to you, every one of them looks like an anonymous bounce. The pattern that should be your loudest buying signal is the one you're completely blind to.

There's a quiet cost to that blindness. When you can't tell a first-time looker from someone on their third pass, you treat everyone the same — which usually means you treat everyone like a stranger and wait for them to make the first move. But the person on their third visit isn't a stranger. They're the closest thing to a sure job on your whole site, and they're the one most likely to go with whoever reaches out while they're still deciding.

## How do you catch a customer who visited three times before calling?

You make the repeat visit visible. With [consent-first identification](/features/multi-channel-follow-up/), a visitor who accepts a clear consent banner becomes a real, consented contact — and when that same person comes back, you recognize them instead of treating every visit as a stranger. A name, an email, a record logged with a timestamp. No form fill, no phone number to cold-call.

Now the second visit isn't a mystery. It's a signal: this person is circling, they're getting closer, and a short, well-timed email — "saw you were back, happy to answer questions" — meets them right in the middle of the decision instead of after a competitor already did.

And it doesn't feel pushy, because the timing is right. You're not interrupting someone who never thought about you; you're reaching out to a person who keeps coming back to your page, offering to help with the exact thing they're already weighing. That's the kind of follow-up homeowners actually welcome — it lands like good service, not a sales pitch, because it arrives at the moment they were quietly looking for a reason to commit.

## Why getting there first decides it

Once you can see the returning shopper, speed wins the job. [78% of homeowners hire the contractor who responds first](https://www.vendasta.com/blog/lead-response-time/), and reaching a lead within five minutes makes you up to [21× more likely to qualify it](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf) than waiting half an hour. Both numbers, with sources, live on our [stats page](/stats/).

The shop that recognizes the repeat visitor and follows up first isn't competing on price by the time the phone rings. It's the one that showed up while the customer was still making up their mind. By the time a slower competitor gets around to a generic "thanks for your interest," the decision is already made — and it was made in favor of the shop that treated the second visit as the buying signal it was.

## What to put in place

- **Turn on consent-first identification** so returning shoppers stop reading as anonymous bounces.
- **Watch for the second visit** as a buying signal, and have a short, friendly email ready for it.
- **Respond first.** Build a same-day follow-up habit so a returning visitor hears from you before they finish comparing.
- **Connect your funnel.** Route recognized contacts into Jobber, Housecall Pro, or HubSpot so the follow-up runs on the system you already use.

Your best customers were never strangers who appeared out of nowhere. They were researching you the whole time. See those visits, follow up first, and a recovered, exclusive lead costs a flat **$7** — a small price to be the shop that showed up before the call.
