## The second visit nobody told you about

Memorial Day weekend is almost here. A homeowner who poked around your site three weeks ago — looked at the work, read a few reviews, didn't reach out — just opened it again on their lunch break. Same person, second visit, days before the cookouts start.

Most shops never know this happened. The visitor came, the visitor went, and the only record is a number in an analytics dashboard you'll never connect to a name. Meanwhile, the strongest buying signal you'll get all month just walked past unnoticed.

## Why a return visit is worth more than the first one

Think about what a second visit actually means. The first time, they were gathering options — your site was one of several tabs. They left, they sat with it, maybe they checked a competitor or two. And then they came *back* to you. That's not idle browsing. That's a homeowner narrowing the field and circling the shop they're leaning toward.

The trouble is timing. Right before a long weekend, people want the deck stained, the yard squared away, the system checked — done before the guests arrive. If you don't know they're back, you're not in the conversation. By the time you'd normally follow up, the cookout-season job is booked with whoever answered first.

## How do you know the moment a past visitor returns?

You let the system watch the door for you. With [multi-channel follow-up](/resources/glossary/#multi-channel-follow-up), when a known, consented visitor comes back to your site, you're alerted — so you can reach out while they're still leaning in. The return itself becomes the trigger.

Because every contact is [consent-first](/features/multi-channel-follow-up/), you're only ever notified about homeowners who accepted a clear banner and have a timestamped record on file. No guessing, no cold list. Just a heads-up that someone who already shortlisted you is back and warm — and a clean, email-grade way to follow up that day.

## Why being first is the whole game

Once you know they're back, speed decides it. [78% of homeowners hire the contractor who responds first](https://www.vendasta.com/blog/lead-response-time/) — not the cheapest, not the most reviewed, the fastest. And the window is tight: reaching a lead within five minutes makes you up to [21× more likely to qualify it](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf) than waiting until after thirty.

The follow-up itself earns attention, too. Recovery emails have been documented recovering around [20% of abandoned visits](https://www.convertcart.com/blog/cart-abandonment-rate-statistics) and pulling open rates near [45%](https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/abandoned-cart-benchmarks) in ecommerce. Those are cross-industry figures, so read them as proof that timely email follow-up works — not a guaranteed result for your shop. Results vary by trade, traffic, and follow-up. Every number is sourced on our [stats page](/stats/).

## The cookout-season clock is short

Timing is what makes the pre-holiday return different from any other week. Most of the year, a returning visitor is a good signal you can act on at a comfortable pace. Right before Memorial Day, that same signal comes with a deadline the homeowner set for themselves: they want it done before the guests show up. The deck stained. The lawn handled. The AC checked so the house is cool for the cookout.

That self-imposed deadline is a gift, because it compresses the decision. A homeowner who'd normally mull a project for a month suddenly wants a "yes" this week. But it cuts both ways — if you're not the one who reaches out while the window's open, somebody else fills it, and the job's gone until the next season rolls around.

So the return visit before a long weekend isn't just warm; it's warm *and* fast-moving. The shops that win it are the ones set up to notice the moment it happens and respond the same hour, while the homeowner is still at their desk thinking about it. Knowing they're back is half the battle. Acting on it before lunch is the other half.

This is also where keeping it consent-first works in your favor rather than against you. You're reaching a homeowner who already said yes to hearing from you, with an email they'll actually open — not a cold interruption they'll resent during a busy holiday week. The relationship is already warm; you're just being prompt and useful.

## What to do before the long weekend

- **Turn on return-visit alerts now.** The pre-holiday window is short; you want to know the same hour someone comes back, not next week.
- **Have a same-day email ready.** Short and warm — "saw you were back; want us to lock in your spot before the weekend?" — beats a generic blast.
- **Route it into your follow-up system.** Send returning-visitor alerts into HubSpot, GoHighLevel, or whatever you run, so the response is fast and consistent every time.

A returning visitor right before Memorial Day is a homeowner with their hand half-raised. Be the first to know they're back, follow up while it's hot, and you book the cookout-season job your competitors never saw coming.
