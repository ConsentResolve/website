## The follow-up that fired an hour too late

Your CRM did everything right. It sent the welcome email, scheduled the reminder, kicked off the sequence. The only problem: the lead reached it ninety minutes after the homeowner first landed on your site — and by then they'd already talked to two other shops and booked one. The automation worked perfectly. It just started the race from the back of the pack.

Most contractors who feel let down by their CRM assume they need faster software. They don't. A CRM can only move as fast as the lead reaches it, and for most shops the lead reaches it slow.

## Why speed is an input, not a setting

You can't tune your way to fast follow-up if the contact shows up late. Picture how leads actually enter your system: a call comes in while you're under a sink and the name gets scribbled on a receipt, entered three days later. A form fill lands at 9 p.m. and nobody sees it until lunch. A visitor leaves your site entirely and never becomes a record at all.

No CRM setting fixes that, because the delay happens *before* the software ever sees the lead. Speed-to-lead isn't a feature you switch on in Jobber or ServiceTitan — it's a property of how the contact got there. Feed it slow, and the fastest follow-up sequence in the world is already losing.

## What's the most important thing to get into a CRM fast?

The contact, complete, the instant it happens — because the clock is the whole game.

The classic [MIT lead-response study](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf) found that contacting a lead within five minutes makes you about **21× more likely** to qualify it than waiting until after thirty. That's not a small edge — that's the difference between a booked job and a voicemail. (Worth saying plainly: that's about how fast you respond, not a promise about any one channel.) But your CRM can only deliver a five-minute response if the lead arrived in *minute one*, not after someone re-typed it off a note. Speed out depends entirely on speed in. You can see that figure, sourced, on our [stats page](/stats/).

## The slowest input of all is the invisible one

There's a lead that's slower than the sticky note, because it never arrives at all: the visitor who looked at your site, didn't fill out a form, and left. To your CRM, that person never existed — which is infinitely slow. They were real, they were interested, and the clock on them ran out before it ever started.

This is where [consent-first identification](/resources/glossary/#consent-first) changes the timing. When a visitor accepts a clear consent banner, that anonymous browser becomes a complete contact — name, consented email, timestamp — and it flows straight into your CRM the moment it happens, no re-typing, no lag. [Clean, instant delivery into the tools you already use](/features/crm-delivery/) is what lets the five-minute follow-up your CRM was built for actually fire on time.

And because it's consent-first, the lead carries a signed receipt and a timestamped consent log — protection for your shop, not just speed. It's exclusive to you, a flat $7, never resold to the contractor down the road.

## Speed and completeness work together

It's tempting to treat speed as the only thing that matters, but a fast lead that's wrong is its own kind of dead end. A contact that lands in minute one but has a typo'd email bounces just as silently as one entered three days late. Speed gets the follow-up to fire; completeness makes sure it lands. You need both, and the reason automated, consented delivery beats hand entry is that it delivers them together — a real, deliverable email *and* the timestamp, instantly, with nobody re-keying anything off a sticky note.

This is also where the kind of CRM you run matters less than people assume. Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan — they're all perfectly capable of firing a five-minute follow-up. What they can't do is invent a lead that reached them slow or arrived broken. So before you go shopping for a faster tool, look at the pipe feeding the one you've got. Fix the input — instant, complete, consented — and the CRM you already pay for will suddenly look a lot smarter than it did last quarter.

## Feed it speed this week

- **Time your slowest lead.** From the moment a homeowner reaches out to the moment your CRM acts — how long? That gap is where jobs die.
- **Wire up automatic, consented delivery** so visitors become complete records the instant they consent — into Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, or whatever you run — with no hand-keying in between.
- **Let the automation fire immediately.** The first responder usually wins, and a CRM fed in real time can be that responder for you.

You don't need a faster CRM. You need to stop handing it leads that are already cold. Get complete, consented contacts in the instant they happen, and the tool you already pay for finally beats the clock.
