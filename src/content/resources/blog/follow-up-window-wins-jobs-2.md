## The week your books want to close

It's mid-December. The homeowner whose furnace has been groaning since Thanksgiving finally decides to deal with it — because the in-laws arrive in ten days and a cold guest room is not the plan. So they grab their phone, search, and click through a few contractor sites, including yours.

They read your reviews. They look at your work. Then they close the tab. And in your slowest-feeling week of the year, a ready buyer just slipped past without leaving a name.

What stings is that this happens during exactly the stretch when a couple of extra jobs would make the month. December and the new year bring a different kind of customer — one with a deadline they can feel — and the shop that catches them goes into January with momentum instead of a cold calendar.

## Why year-end leads don't wait

Most of the year, a homeowner browsing your site might sit on a decision for weeks. At year-end, they won't. They want the work done before the holidays, before the year-end deadline they've set in their own head, before the house fills up. That urgency is good news — except it also means they'll book the *first* pro who gets back to them and move on.

That's the trap. The lead is hotter than ever, but the [follow-up window](/resources/glossary/#follow-up-window) — the time you have to actually reach them before they commit to someone else — is shorter than ever. Miss it by a day and the job is gone, not because your price was wrong, but because you were second.

It helps to picture the window for what it is. A homeowner decides to act, opens a few tabs, and starts a mental clock. Whoever lands a real, helpful reply inside that clock gets the conversation; everyone who shows up after it runs out is talking to someone who's already half-committed elsewhere. The rest of the year that clock might run for days. At year-end it can run out by dinner.

## How fast do you have to follow up to win a year-end job?

Faster than you think, and the data is blunt about it. [78% of homeowners hire the contractor who responds first](https://www.vendasta.com/blog/lead-response-time/) — not the cheapest, not the most reviewed, the fastest. And contacting a lead within five minutes makes you up to [21× more likely to qualify it](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf) than waiting until after thirty. Both figures sit on our [stats page](/stats/), each one sourced.

In December, those numbers bite harder. The homeowner racing the holidays isn't going to leave a voicemail and wait. They'll take the first real answer they get.

## The problem most shops never see

Here's the catch: you can't answer a lead you never knew existed. The homeowner who priced a furnace replacement Tuesday night and didn't fill out your form is invisible to you — so there's nothing to respond *to*, fast or slow.

That's the gap [consent-first identification](/features/multi-channel-follow-up/) closes. When a visitor accepts a clear consent banner, an anonymous browser becomes a real, consented contact — a name and an email, logged with a timestamp. No form fill, no phone number to cold-call. Now there's a lead to work, and a window you can actually beat.

Think of it as the difference between a storefront with a guest book and one without. The homeowner who priced a furnace replacement Tuesday night and didn't call hasn't decided against you — they just walked out without signing in. Recover that consented contact and you can send one short, helpful note Wednesday morning, while their problem is still cold and their decision is still open. You're not chasing strangers. You're following up with someone who was already standing in your shop.

## What to do before the holidays close your year

- **Turn on consent-first follow-up now**, while year-end search traffic is high, so the December rush doesn't leak away anonymous.
- **Keep one short email ready** — "Want us to get that handled before the holidays?" — so recovered visitors hear from you the same day, not next week.
- **Be first.** Build a quick morning and end-of-day check into the routine so no lead sits overnight during the weeks it matters most.
- **Lean on the funnel you already run.** Pipe recovered contacts into Jobber, Housecall Pro, or HubSpot and let your existing follow-up do the rest.

You don't need a bigger ad budget to finish the year strong. You need to answer the ready buyers already on your site before the window shuts. A recovered, consented lead costs a flat **$7**, exclusive to you and never resold — cheap leverage on a job someone else is about to book.

The year is closing. The buyers are in a hurry. Be the one who answers first, and the job is yours.
