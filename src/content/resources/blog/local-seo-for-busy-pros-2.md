## What "local SEO" even means

Somebody told you that you need "local SEO," and it sounded like a thing you'd have to go back to school for. You don't. Strip away the jargon and here's the whole idea: local SEO is the work that helps your business show up when someone close by searches for what you do — "AC repair near me," "fence company in [town]," "plumber open now."

That's it. It's not a secret algorithm you have to outsmart. It's mostly two plain things: making it easy for Google to understand who you are and where you work, and making it easy for a homeowner to trust you in the few seconds they spend deciding. Everything useful about local SEO comes back to those two.

## Why this matters more for you than for a big company

Most SEO advice online is written for companies competing nationally, where tiny technical tweaks matter because they're fighting the whole internet. You're not. You're competing with the handful of other shops in your county, for a homeowner standing in their kitchen who needs someone *today*.

That changes what wins. You don't need a perfect technical score. You need to be the obvious, trustworthy, nearby choice the moment someone in your area searches. Being close, being clearly real, and being easy to contact beats a hundred points of behind-the-scenes optimization that a homeowner never sees.

## What actually moves the needle when you're slammed?

A short list does most of the work. Get these right and you can ignore almost everything else the SEO crowd tries to sell you.

- **Finish your Google Business Profile.** For most local searches this matters more than your website. Right categories, your real service area, current hours, real photos, and a few posts now and then. It's free, and it's the single biggest lever.
- **Keep your business details the same everywhere.** Your name, address, and phone should match exactly across your site, your profile, and any listing. Mismatches confuse search engines and make you look careless to homeowners.
- **Keep reviews coming.** Recent, real reviews are local SEO and sales proof in one. Don't chase them in awkward bursts — bake the ask into your follow-up so it happens every job. (More in [getting reviews without begging](/resources/blog/get-more-reviews-without-begging/).)
- **Write pages the way people search.** A clear page for each main service, and for each town where it fits. Say "metal roof repair in [town]," not industry jargon nobody types.

That's the bulk of it. The rest is polish you can get to later, or never.

## The part nobody mentions until it's too late

Here's where even good advice stops short. Ranking gets people *to* your site. It does nothing about what happens once they're there — and for most of them, what happens is they look around and leave without a word. You won the search, earned the click, and still don't know who they were.

That's the gap. Being found is only half the job; traffic that leaks straight back out doesn't pay for itself. [Consent-first visitor identification](/features/visitor-identification/) closes it: when a visitor accepts a clear consent banner, you get a real, consented contact — a name and an email — for the people who would otherwise vanish. No form fill, then follow-up by email into the funnel you already run, never a cold call. A recovered lead is a flat **$7**, exclusive to you, never resold.

Search fills the top of the funnel. Capture keeps it from draining out the bottom. You need both, and the second is where most pros are quietly leaving money behind.

## What about all the stuff I'm "supposed" to do?

If you've read any local SEO advice, you've seen the long lists — schema markup, backlink building, citation cleanup, blog cadences, keyword density. Here's the honest take: most of it is real, and almost none of it matters for a working contractor until the basics are solid. Those tactics squeeze small edges in tight national races. You're not in a national race. You're trying to be the obvious nearby choice in one county, and the four items above do the overwhelming majority of that work.

So don't feel guilty about skipping the rest. The contractor who keeps a great Google profile, consistent details, fresh reviews, and clear service pages will out-rank the one who paid an agency to chase the other hundred points but let their hours go stale. Save the advanced stuff for when you've genuinely outgrown the fundamentals — which, for most pros, is later than the SEO industry would like you to believe. Plain beats fancy, and done beats perfect.

## Your short, busy-person to-do list

- **Finish your Google Business Profile this week** — categories, photos, service area, hours. Biggest free lever you have.
- **Make your business details match everywhere** and build out a plain page per service and town.
- **Capture the traffic you earn** so ranking turns into booked jobs instead of anonymous visits — and check how it compares to your other [lead channels](/resources/compare/). When a term trips you up, the [marketing glossary](/resources/glossary/) keeps it in plain English.

Skip the busywork. Do the short list well, capture what it sends you, and you'll book more than the pro still color-coding a spreadsheet.
