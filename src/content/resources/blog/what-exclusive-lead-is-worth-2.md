## "I won the job — but at what price?"

Plenty of contractors track how many leads turn into jobs. Fewer track what those jobs were *worth* by the time they closed. Because here's the thing about a shared lead: even when you win it, you usually win it cheaper than you should have — and that quiet discount is the real cost nobody puts on the invoice.

The price on a lead tells you almost nothing about what it's worth to your shop. What it's worth depends on a quieter question: how much margin survives the fight to win it?

## The shared-lead squeeze on your margin

On the big lead-seller platforms, the same project gets sold to multiple contractors. A shared lead commonly runs [$25 to $100 or more](https://www.homeservicedirect.net/how-to-get-leads-flooring-business/), and that single prospect typically lands with [4–5 pros](https://www.homeservicedirect.net/how-to-get-leads-flooring-business/) at once. Now picture the homeowner: five quotes in their inbox, all for the same job. What's the fastest way to stand out in that pile? Cut your price.

So the shared lead does two kinds of damage. Most of the time you lose the bidding war and eat the lead fee for nothing. And the times you *win*, you often win by being the cheapest — which means the job that finally closed is carrying a thinner margin than it should. Even the blended cost-per-lead on the major paid ad channel sits around [$53](https://searchlightdigital.io/google-local-service-ads-cost-per-lead/), and at least that channel doesn't resell the same lead five times over.

## So what is exclusivity actually worth?

It's worth the discount you *don't* have to give.

An [exclusive lead](/resources/glossary/#exclusive-lead) is sold to you and no one else. The homeowner isn't holding four other quotes; they came to your site, on their own, and you're the only one following up. So you're not competing on being the lowest — you're competing on being the right call. You quote on value, the conversation starts warm instead of weary, and the margin you build into the price actually makes it to the bank.

That's why a cheap exclusive lead can be worth far more to your shop than an expensive shared one. You're not buying a name. You're buying the absence of a bidding war — and the profit that survives because of it.

## The math when you run it across a season

A single lead is easy to wave off either way. The difference shows up when you zoom out to a season. Say you'd otherwise buy fifty shared leads to land your jobs, at a blended $53 apiece — that's $2,650, before counting the ones you discounted to win and the office hours spent chasing homeowners who'd already heard from four other shops. Now run the same volume of exclusive recovery leads at $7 each: $350, with no bidding war and no pressure to undercut your own quote.

Even if the shared leads booked at a higher raw rate — which they usually don't, because four competitors are racing you — the margin you keep on the exclusive jobs changes the whole picture. You're not just spending less per lead; you're keeping more of every job you win, because none of those wins came from being the cheapest. That's the compounding part the sticker price never shows. Over a year, the gap between "won it on price" and "won it on value" is often larger than the entire lead bill itself.

## Put a number on it before you pay

- **Count the discount, not just the win.** Estimate how much you shave off a quote to beat a stack of competing bids. That number is the hidden cost of every shared lead you win.
- **Separate price from exclusivity.** A $7 exclusive lead and a $75 shared one aren't the same product at different prices. They're different products — one comes with a bidding war, one doesn't.
- **Value the margin you keep.** A lead that lets you quote on value instead of price protects profit on every job it produces. That's what exclusivity is worth to your shop — and at a flat $7, it stops looking like the budget option and starts looking like the obvious one.

The lead-seller pitch leans on the sticker. The real question is quieter: when this job finally closes, how much of it did you keep? That answer is what an exclusive lead is actually worth.
