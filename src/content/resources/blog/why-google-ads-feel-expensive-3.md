## You're not mad at the click price

Ask a contractor why Google Ads feel expensive and you'll hear the same answer: "The clicks cost a fortune." So they lower bids, pause campaigns, fight the keyword report. And the bill barely moves, because the click was never the problem.

Here's the uncomfortable math. You pay for every impression that turns into a click. You do *not* get a lead from most of those clicks. The gap between "what you paid for" and "what turned into a real contact" is the expensive part — and it's hiding after the click, where your dashboard doesn't show it.

## Where the money actually leaks

Run the channel honestly. The blended [average Local Services Ads cost per lead is about $53](https://searchlightdigital.io/google-local-service-ads-cost-per-lead/), and per-trade it climbs — [HVAC $45–$85, Plumbing $35–$65, Electrical $35–$70, Roofing $50–$95](https://www.homeservicedirect.net/local-service-ads-for-contractors/). Those are the leads you can see. They feel costly on their own.

But that's only the visible bill. For every paid click that becomes a tracked lead, a pile of other paid clicks land on your site and leave. Across home-service websites, roughly [98% of visitors never convert or identify themselves](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/conversion-rate-benchmarks). You paid for those clicks. You paid for the impressions that earned them. And then they walked out the door anonymous.

Think about what you're really buying when you buy an impression. Google charges you to put your ad in front of a homeowner. Most of those homeowners scroll past — fine, you usually don't pay for that. But the ones who click are the ones who raised their hand, the most valuable people in the whole auction. Those are exactly the clicks you pay top dollar for. And then your own site, the place they landed, has no way to keep them. You won the auction for the best prospects in your market and then let them slip out the back. That's not a Google problem. That's a capture problem wearing a Google bill.

## What is your real cost per booked job?

This is the number that matters, and almost nobody calculates it. Your real cost isn't [cost per click](/resources/glossary/#cost-per-lead) — it's your total ad spend divided by the jobs you actually book. When most of your paid traffic leaves without becoming a lead, that denominator stays small while the bill stays large. The effective cost per job balloons, even though your cost per click looks perfectly reasonable.

So you do the natural thing: assume you need *more* traffic to get more jobs, and pour more money into the same leaky funnel. The clicks were never the issue. The wasted impressions and the lost-after-click visitors were.

## The leak is recoverable

Here's the part that changes the math. The visitors who clicked your ad and left aren't gone — they just left without telling you who they are. With [consent-first visitor identification](/features/visitor-identification/), a visitor who accepts a clear consent banner becomes a real, consented contact: a name and an email-grade lead, logged with a timestamp. No form fill, no phone number to cold-call — follow-up is email, into the funnel you already run.

That means a click you already paid for at full price doesn't have to evaporate. The homeowner who clicked your roofing ad, browsed two pages, and bounced can hear from you the same afternoon — without you spending another dollar on ads.

And it stacks cleanly on top of what you already run. Recovered contacts flow into the same email tool and CRM you use today — Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, HubSpot — so this isn't a new system to learn. It's a patch on the leak in the system you've already built and paid for. The ad still runs the same way. The click still costs the same. The only thing that changes is that the visitor who used to vanish now becomes someone you can follow up with.

## Why this beats lowering your bid

Cutting your bid lowers cost per click and lowers your traffic at the same time. You're shrinking the top of the funnel to save pennies. Recovering lost clicks does the opposite: it pulls more booked jobs out of the *exact same* traffic you're already buying.

And the recovery price is the kicker. A paid LSA lead averages [$53](https://searchlightdigital.io/google-local-service-ads-cost-per-lead/). Recovering a homeowner who was already on your site costs a flat **$7**, and that lead is exclusive to you — never resold to the three competitors bidding on the same keyword. You can see how that stacks up against every channel on our [stats page](/stats/), each figure sourced.

## What to do this quarter

- **Stop optimizing the click and start measuring cost per job.** Divide your real ad spend by booked jobs. That's the number that's actually expensive.
- **Turn on consent-first identification** so the paid clicks that don't convert get recovered instead of lost.
- **Have one same-day email ready** for recovered visitors — short and helpful — so the click you paid for finishes the journey.
- **Compare your channels honestly.** Before adding budget, see what a booked job really costs you on each one with our [channel comparison](/resources/compare/).

Your Google Ads aren't expensive because clicks cost too much. They're expensive because you're paying full freight for impressions and clicks that quietly disappear. Plug that leak and the bill you already pay starts working a lot harder.
