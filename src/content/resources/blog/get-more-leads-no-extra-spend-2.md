## The leads you already paid for are leaving

When a contractor tells me they need more leads, the first thing they reach for is the ad budget. Spend more on Google, spend more on the platforms, push harder. It feels like the obvious move.

But there's a cheaper batch of leads sitting right under it — the people already on your site today. You paid to get them there. Most of them are walking out the back before you ever learn their name.

## Spending more on a leaky funnel

Here's why turning up the ad budget often disappoints. Across home-service websites, roughly [98% of visitors never convert or identify themselves](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/conversion-rate-benchmarks). They land, they look, they leave anonymous.

So when you double your ad spend, you don't double your leads. You double the *traffic* — and 98% of the new traffic leaks out the same hole as the old. That's the [leaky bucket](/resources/glossary/#leaky-bucket): pouring more water in the top doesn't help much when the bottom's wide open. The smart first move isn't a bigger tap. It's a smaller hole.

## So where do free leads come from?

Not free, exactly — but close to it, because you've already covered the expensive part. Getting a homeowner to your site is what costs money: the ads, the SEO work, the time. Once they're there, the marginal cost of *keeping* one is tiny compared to acquiring a brand-new visitor.

That's the opportunity. The visitors are already arriving. The only question is whether you have a way to hold onto the ones who don't fill out a form — which is nearly all of them.

## The evidence: capture pays back more than clicks

This is where it gets convincing. In ecommerce, where returns on this are tracked closely, identifying visitors and acting on that data has delivered [6–10× ROI](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/how-brands-boost-roi-with-smart-data). That's cross-industry evidence, not a promise for your shop — results vary by trade, traffic, and follow-up — but it tells you where the leverage lives. The money isn't in buying more strangers. It's in not losing the visitors you already paid to attract.

Apply that to a contractor site and the picture's the same: the cheapest lead you'll ever get is one who's already on your page. The numbers are sourced on our [stats page](/stats/).

## How to grow leads on your current budget

- **Audit the leak first.** Before adding ad spend, ask what happens to the 98% who land and leave. If the answer is "nothing," that's your growth.
- **Turn on consent-first capture.** When a visitor accepts a clear consent banner, that consented visitor becomes a real name and email — no form, no phone number to cold-call.
- **Follow up by email, same day.** One short, helpful message into your existing funnel beats a louder ad every time.
- **Route it to your CRM.** Send recovered contacts into Jobber, Housecall Pro, or HubSpot so follow-up runs without you remembering to.

You may not need a bigger budget at all. You need to keep more of what your current one already brings you.
