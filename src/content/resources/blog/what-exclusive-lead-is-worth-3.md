## What are you really buying?

Every contractor knows the price of a lead. Fewer stop to ask what they're actually getting for it. On the big lead-seller platforms, the answer is uncomfortable: you're not buying a customer. You're buying the *right to compete* for one — alongside everybody else who bought the same lead.

That distinction is the whole ballgame. And once you see it, the way you value a lead changes for good.

## The hidden cost of a shared lead

Shared leads on seller platforms run [$25–$100+ each](https://pipelineon.com/blog/is-thumbtack-worth-it/). That alone stings. But the price tag isn't the real cost — the sharing is. The same lead typically goes to [4–5 pros](https://pipelineon.com/blog/how-much-does-thumbtack-charge-per-lead/), all of whom call the same homeowner within minutes of each other.

So picture it from the homeowner's side: their phone rings five times, five quotes land, and they pick one. Your odds of being that one are, on average, about one in five — and you paid full price for the privilege. Even the channel that doesn't share leads, Local Services Ads, blends out to [$53 per lead](https://searchlightdigital.io/google-local-service-ads-cost-per-lead/). A shared lead's true cost is the price *divided by your real close rate*, and that close rate is dragged down by every other pro holding the same lead.

## So what is an exclusive lead actually worth?

An [exclusive lead](/resources/glossary/#exclusive-lead) is sold to one contractor — you — and never resold. No race. No five-way phone tag. The homeowner hears from you, and the conversation is yours to win or lose on your own merits.

That changes the math in your favor twice. Your close rate climbs because you're not splitting the homeowner's attention four ways. And your margin holds because you're not forced into a price war just to be the cheapest of five quotes. The same job, won without the bidding pit, is simply worth more to your shop.

For a fuller side-by-side, the [shared-vs-exclusive breakdown](/resources/blog/shared-vs-exclusive-leads-math/) walks the numbers, and the [comparison hub](/resources/compare/) stacks the platforms up.

## Run your own number

Here's the only calculation that matters. Take your average job value. Multiply by your honest close rate on the lead. Subtract the lead cost. That's what the lead is worth to *you*.

Now run it twice. A shared lead at, say, $60 with a one-in-five close rate looks very different once you divide. An exclusive lead — yours alone, higher close rate, at a flat **$7** — comes out ahead long before you count the hours you didn't spend chasing a homeowner who already booked someone else. Every figure here is sourced on our [stats page](/stats/), and you can see exclusive pricing laid out on our [pricing page](/pricing/).

## The cost the spreadsheet leaves out

There's one more line that never makes it into the cost-per-lead math, and it's a big one: your time. Chasing a shared lead means racing four other pros to the phone, leaving voicemails, sending texts that may never get answered — because the homeowner is fielding the same scramble from everyone else. A lot of that effort produces nothing, and effort that produces nothing is still effort you paid for in hours you don't have.

An exclusive lead is calmer by design. There's no race, so there's no frantic five-minute window where you're competing to be the first of five. You reach out, you have a real conversation, and you either win it on the merits or you don't — but you're not burning a morning on a homeowner who booked someone before you finished dialing. For a shop where the owner is also the estimator and half the crew, that recovered time is worth real money. The exclusive lead doesn't just close better; it costs you less to work.

## So what's the right way to think about lead value?

Stop treating "cost per lead" as the headline number. The number that actually runs your business is cost per *job* — what you spent to land one paying customer, all in. A cheap shared lead with a low close rate and a high time cost can quietly carry a brutal cost per job. An exclusive lead at a flat **$7**, closed at a higher rate with less wasted effort, usually carries a far lower one.

That's the whole case for exclusivity in one sentence: you're not paying for a lead, you're paying for the cleanest possible path to a job — and a path you don't have to share is worth more than a crowded one, even when it costs a fraction as much. Recovered from a visitor who was already on your site, consent-first and never resold, an exclusive lead is about as efficient as a lead gets.

## What to do this week

- **Stop comparing leads by sticker price.** Compare them by what you keep after close rate and time.
- **Tally your shared-lead close rate honestly.** If you're winning one in five, your real cost per job is five times the lead price.
- **Value exclusivity.** No competing quotes means a higher close and a held margin — that's where the worth lives.
- **Follow up first.** Even with an exclusive lead, the fastest, most helpful response wins the work.

A cheap lead that five pros are quoting isn't cheap. An exclusive lead at a flat **$7** — yours alone, never resold, recovered from a visitor already on your site — is worth more than its price suggests, because you keep almost all of it.
