## The problem we set out to fix

Here's the number that started it all: about **98% of the homeowners who land on your website leave without filling out a form.** You paid for every one of those visits — through Google, through Meta, through the referral you earned the hard way. They showed up, they looked around, and then they were gone. No name. No number. Nothing to follow up on.

Most shops try to fix that by buying *more* traffic. That's treating a leak like a supply problem. The visitors aren't the issue — the leak is. The homeowners who need you are already showing up. You just never get to meet the ones who don't raise their hand.

## What Consent Resolve actually does

Consent Resolve is a **consent-first visitor-identification layer.** In plain terms: when a homeowner on your site accepts a clear consent banner, that "yes" becomes permission to reach out — and we hand you their name and a real email as a lead.

Three things make it different from anything you've been sold:

- **It's exclusive.** The lead comes off *your* website and goes to *you*. It isn't the same lead sold to four other contractors an hour later.
- **The permission is proven, not assumed.** Every lead arrives with a timestamped consent record attached — a signed receipt showing who agreed, to what, and when. If a homeowner ever replies "where did you get my email?", you have a clean, dated answer instead of a scramble.
- **It's a flat $7.** No auction, no $50-to-$100-plus shared-lead invoice, no surprise. Seven dollars, and the lead is yours to keep.

You follow up by email, on consent you can stand behind. It's the calm, defensible version of lead-gen — not the sketchy version.

## Why not just buy shared leads?

Because you already know how that story goes. The shared-lead resellers — Thumbtack, Angi, HomeAdvisor — sell the same homeowner to several pros, so you're racing four other shops to the phone on a lead none of you own. And when you ask where the person's information came from, the answer is usually a shrug. That gap is not hypothetical: the FTC's action against HomeAdvisor **settled with an order to pay up to \$7.2 million** over how those leads were marketed.

Consent-first is the opposite posture. The homeowner opted in on your site, the record proves it, and the lead belongs to you alone. Same ad budget, a lead you can actually defend.
