## The metric that feels like winning

Traffic is the number everyone wants to talk about. It goes up, the dashboard turns green, the agency sends a screenshot. It *feels* like progress.

But ask the only question that pays the bills — how many jobs did it book? — and traffic alone has no answer. For most contractors, chasing more of it is aiming at the wrong target entirely.

## Why volume hides the real problem

Here's what's actually happening on your site. Across home-service websites, about [98% of visitors never convert or identify themselves](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/conversion-rate-benchmarks), and the average visit lasts roughly [87 seconds](https://spectruminfinite.com/blogs/average-time-spent-on-website-2025/). Under a minute and a half, then gone.

So picture doubling your traffic. You don't double your leads — you double the *anonymous*. Twice as many people land, look around for 87 seconds, and leave without telling you who they are. The leak scales right along with the volume. That's why the owner who triples his clicks often books the same number of jobs and can't figure out why.

## What should I be measuring instead?

Capture rate — the share of visitors who turn into a real, followable contact. It's the difference between a busy storefront and a busy storefront that actually rings up sales.

When capture is broken, every traffic gain is wasted: you're paying to fill a [leaky bucket](/resources/glossary/#leaky-bucket). When capture works, the traffic you *already* have suddenly counts, and every new visitor on top of it counts too. Fix the leak first, and only then does turning up the volume make sense.

## The deeper read on wasted traffic

If you've been buying clicks and wondering where the jobs went, my colleague's piece on [paying for traffic and throwing it away](https://consentresolve.com/resources/blog/paying-for-traffic-throwing-it-away/) walks through exactly how the money leaks — it's the longer companion to this one, and worth your ten minutes. The short version: the spend gets people to the site, the site has no way to hold the ones who don't fill out a form, and the budget evaporates. Every figure here is sourced on our [stats page](/stats/).

## How to make traffic actually count

- **Reframe the goal.** Stop tracking visits as the win. Track how many visitors became contacts you can follow up with.
- **Turn on consent-first capture.** When a visitor accepts a clear consent banner, that consented visitor becomes a real name and email — no form, no phone number to cold-call.
- **Follow up fast, by email.** Have one short, friendly message ready so recovered visitors hear from you the same day they visited.
- **Then scale traffic.** Once you're capturing a real share, more visitors finally translate into more booked jobs. Order matters.

More traffic isn't a goal — it's a cost. The goal is keeping more of what you already attract. Get the capture right and the traffic you have today is worth more than twice the traffic you have now.
