## The tab full of names nobody worked

You know the spreadsheet. It started as a quick place to drop leads — a name here, an email there, "I'll move these into the CRM tonight." Then a real week happened. Now there are forty rows, half of them weeks old, and you can't tell which ones got a follow-up and which ones never heard from anybody at all.

That tab is a graveyard. Leads go in. Most don't come out. And every one that didn't make it into your actual follow-up system was a job you paid to attract and then quietly let go.

## Why the hand-off is where leads die

The problem isn't getting leads. It's the gap between *having* a lead and *working* one. A spreadsheet doesn't remind you to call. It doesn't fire an email sequence. It doesn't flag what's been touched. It just holds names until a human re-types them into the [CRM](/resources/glossary/#crm) — and that human is also running jobs, answering the phone, and quoting work.

So the copy-paste step slips. Then it gets batched to "later." Then later never comes, and a fresh, ready homeowner sits in a tab while a competitor who answered books the job. Manual entry also drags in typos — a fat-fingered email bounces, and the lead's dead on arrival.

## How does a lead get into your CRM without anyone re-typing it?

The fix is to remove the human copy step entirely. With [CRM delivery](/features/crm-delivery/), each recovered, consented visitor is sent straight into the system you already run — [Consent Resolve](/features/crm-delivery/) maps the name, email, and consent record to the right fields, and the lead lands ready to work.

It drops into the CRM your team already lives in — Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, HubSpot, Klaviyo, or GoHighLevel — so nobody's learning a new tool or babysitting a spreadsheet. The lead arrives where your follow-up already happens, not in a holding pen beside it. No re-typing, no bounce-prone typos, no "I'll do it tonight."

## Why a clean hand-off books more jobs

Getting the lead in fast and clean isn't just tidy — it's how jobs get won. [78% of homeowners hire the contractor who responds first](https://pipelineon.com/blog/how-much-does-thumbtack-charge-per-lead/), and reaching a lead within five minutes gives you [21× better odds of qualifying it](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf) than waiting past thirty.

You can't respond first to a lead that's still sitting in a spreadsheet waiting to be typed in. When the hand-off is instant and the lead lands in your CRM ready for its sequence, fast follow-up is actually possible. These numbers shift by trade, traffic, and how you follow up — but the direction never does: leads worked sooner, from a clean record, book more work. The figures are on our [stats page](/stats/).

## How to keep your hand-off clean

- **Pick one system of record.** Whatever CRM you run, make it the only place leads live — and retire the side spreadsheet.
- **Let delivery do the entry.** Send recovered visitors straight in, mapped to fields, so no one re-types a name.
- **Build the first follow-up where the lead lands.** A short, friendly email sequence that fires inside your CRM means new leads start working the moment they arrive.
- **Check for orphans monthly.** Make sure nothing's slipping into an old tab out of habit; if it is, route it in.

A lead that lands clean in your CRM is a lead you can actually work. One that sits in a spreadsheet is a maybe. Close that gap and you book jobs you were already paying to attract.
