## The call you didn't hear

You're elbow-deep in a panel, or on a roof, or driving with the radio up. The phone buzzes once and you miss it. No big deal — they left a voicemail, you'll call back at lunch, right?

Except they didn't leave a voicemail. They hung up and called the next contractor on the search results. By lunch, the job's booked. Not by you. A missed call feels like a missed message. It's actually a job quietly changing hands while your phone sits silent in your pocket.

## Why a missed call is worse than it sounds

Here's the hard number: about [78% of homeowners hire the contractor who responds first](https://www.vendasta.com/blog/lead-response-time/) — not the best-reviewed, not the cheapest, the *first*. You can see that on our [stats page](/stats/). When you don't pick up, you're not "next in line." You've handed the front of the line to whoever did pick up.

And the window is brutal. The classic [MIT lead-response study](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf) found that contacting a lead within five minutes makes you about **21× more likely** to qualify it than waiting until after thirty. A homeowner's intent has a half-life measured in minutes, not days. The "I'll call back at lunch" plan loses to a competitor who answered in the moment.

## The expensive fix everyone reaches for first

So contractors do the obvious thing: hire someone to answer the phone. A receptionist, an answering service, a virtual assistant. That works — and it's costly. Payroll, training, a desk, and they still can't catch the people who never called at all, only ones who looked at your site and left.

The thing is, the goal was never "answer every call live." The goal is "make sure no interested person goes unanswered." Those are different problems, and the second one has a much cheaper solution.

## What's the cheapest way to stop losing jobs to missed calls?

Make the follow-up automatic and fast, across more than one channel — so when you can't pick up, the homeowner still hears from you before they've moved on.

This is where [multi-channel follow-up](/resources/glossary/#multi-channel-follow-up) earns its keep. Two things happen. First, the people who visit your site and *don't even call* — the silent majority — get captured when they accept a clear consent banner, turning into a real contact instead of vanishing. Second, [the system follows up for you](/features/multi-channel-follow-up/) — a fast, friendly email into a funnel they agreed to — so a missed connection doesn't become a lost job.

It's consent-first and email-grade, which matters: you're never cold-calling a number off a list. You're reaching someone who said it's okay, with a timestamped consent record on file. The lead is exclusive to you, a flat $7, never resold to the contractor who *did* answer their phone today. Compare that to a receptionist's salary and the math isn't close.

And it pairs with how people actually buy. A lot of your best customers [visit two or three times before they ever call](/resources/blog/three-visits-before-they-called/) — automatic follow-up keeps you in front of them across those visits instead of relying on them catching you live on the one attempt they make.

## Plug the leak this week

- **Stop treating missed calls as messages.** They're jobs in motion. Assume the homeowner is already dialing the next name.
- **Capture the silent visitors too.** Most interested people never call at all — consent-first identification turns them into contacts you can reach.
- **Automate a fast follow-up.** A same-minute email beats a same-day callback. Be the first response, every time, without adding payroll.

You'll always miss some calls — that's the job. What you don't have to miss is the *lead*. Catch the follow-up fast and cheap, and the busy signal stops costing you work.
