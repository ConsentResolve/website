## The audit you keep meaning to do

You built your website, or paid someone to, and then you got busy doing the actual work. That's how it should be. But a website isn't a truck you buy once and drive for ten years — it drifts. Hours go stale after a holiday. A plugin update quietly breaks the tap-to-call link. A page that loaded fine last spring is crawling now. And because nobody complains — they just leave — you don't find out until a slow season makes you go looking.

The fix isn't a bigger project. It's a smaller, recurring one: a 15-minute check you run on the same calendar reminder every quarter.

## Why once a year isn't enough

A once-a-year audit sounds responsible, and it's better than never. But think about what "once a year" actually means: a problem that appears in January doesn't get caught until the following January. That's a full peak season — your busiest, most expensive-to-reach traffic — leaking out the bottom while you never know.

Quarterly turns that into, at worst, a three-month leak. Four short looks a year cost you about an hour total and catch the slow drift before it eats a whole season of ready buyers. The average visit lasts about [87 seconds](https://spectruminfinite.com/blogs/average-time-spent-on-website-2025/) — you don't get a second chance to make a stale page look fast.

## What should I check on my website each quarter?

Run these the same way every time, on your phone, like a homeowner deciding whether to hire you. Same list, four times a year — that's the point.

- **Does it still load fast?** Re-time it. Pages get heavier as you add photos and tools. If you're tapping your foot, so are they.
- **Is your phone number tappable and obvious?** Updates break links. Confirm it's at the top of every page and actually dials when tapped.
- **Are your hours and service area current?** This is the one that drifts most. Stale holiday hours or a town you no longer cover quietly sends people elsewhere.
- **Is there one clear next step per page?** Redesigns and add-ons pile up competing buttons. Confused buyers don't choose; they leave.
- **Are your reviews and photos recent?** A homeowner comparing you to the next [search result](/resources/blog/local-seo-for-busy-pros-2/) wants proof you're still active and good. Swap in this quarter's best job.
- **Are the visitors who don't call still being captured?** The biggest leak, and the one worth re-confirming every single quarter.

## The leak worth rechecking every time

Here's the part a one-time audit gets wrong: even a fast, clean, current site loses most of its ready buyers. Across home-service sites, roughly [98% of visitors never convert or identify themselves](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/conversion-rate-benchmarks) — they look around, get what they need, and leave without a form or a call. You did everything right and still don't know they were there.

That's not a friction you fix once; it's a leak you keep plugged. [Consent-first visitor identification](/features/visitor-identification/) handles it: when a visitor accepts a clear consent banner, you get a real, consented contact — a name and an email — for the people who would otherwise vanish. No form fill, then follow-up by email into the funnel you already run, never a cold call. A recovered lead is a flat **$7**, exclusive to you, never resold. Each quarter, just confirm it's still on and still firing.

## Why a checklist beats a redesign

When something feels off about their website, a lot of contractors reach for the nuclear option: a full rebuild. It's expensive, it takes months, and most of the time it fixes problems the old site didn't actually have while introducing fresh ones. A quarterly checklist is the opposite bet. It assumes your site is mostly fine and just needs small, regular maintenance — the same way you'd rather change the oil four times a year than replace the engine once.

The checklist also builds something a redesign can't: a baseline. After two or three quarters of running the same six checks, you start to notice patterns — the page that always slows down after you add photos, the listing that drifts out of sync every time you update hours. You stop reacting to problems and start heading them off. And because the whole thing takes about fifteen minutes, it actually gets done, which is more than most expensive audits can say. The best maintenance is the kind cheap and quick enough that you never skip it.

## Put it on the calendar

- **Set a recurring 15-minute reminder** — first week of each quarter — and run the checklist above on your phone every time.
- **Fix what drifted since last quarter first** — speed, the phone link, stale hours. Quick wins, no agency required.
- **Confirm the anonymous-visitor leak is plugged** so a season's worth of ready buyers don't disappear, then sanity-check what your other [lead channels](/resources/compare/) really cost.

The best audit isn't the most thorough one you do once. It's the short one you actually run again — every quarter, on your own site, the way your next customer will. Every figure here is sourced on our [stats page](/stats/).
