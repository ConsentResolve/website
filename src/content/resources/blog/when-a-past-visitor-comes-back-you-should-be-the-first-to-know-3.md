## The name that went quiet — and just lit back up

Every shop has a list of homeowners who looked once and disappeared. They priced the job, maybe started a quote, and then went dark. You wrote them off, or you never knew they were there to write off in the first place.

Then one of them comes back. Three weeks later, on a Thursday night, they're on your site again — and this time they're not skimming the homepage. They're on the financing page, or back in the photo gallery, or reading the one service page that matches their exact problem. That reappearance is not random traffic. It's a buying-intent spike, and it's the single best moment you'll get to win the job.

## Why most shops sleep through the spike

The problem isn't that contractors don't follow up. It's that they follow up on a flat schedule — Monday batch, end-of-week catch-up — while buying intent is anything but flat. It sits near zero for weeks, then jumps the hour a homeowner decides they're ready. If your follow-up rhythm doesn't bend toward that jump, you're sending your best message at an average moment and your average message at the best moment.

That's the leaky bucket at its most expensive point. The hole isn't at the top, where strangers wander in. It's right at the bottom, where a homeowner who already knows you, already trusts you enough to return, slips out because nobody noticed the spike while it was happening.

## How do you catch a buying-intent spike in time?

You get told the moment it happens. That's the job of consent-first [multi-channel follow-up](/resources/glossary/#multi-channel-follow-up). A homeowner who accepted your consent banner on that first visit is a known, opted-in contact. When they return, [Consent Resolve](/features/multi-channel-follow-up/) flags the return as it occurs — not days later in a report — and tells you enough about it to respond well. No phone number, no cold-calling. Just a timely, email-grade nudge into the funnel you already run.

The alert lands in the system you already work out of — [Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, HubSpot, Klaviyo, or GoHighLevel](/features/multi-channel-follow-up/) — so a spike becomes a one-tap action instead of a note you mean to get to. Some homeowners take [three visits before they call](/resources/blog/three-visits-before-they-called/); the alert lets you act on the spike instead of waiting for a call that may never come.

## What to send, and when

The "when" is simple: now. The "what" depends on where the spike pointed.

- **They came back to pricing or financing** → send the cost conversation. "Saw you were back — want a firm number and the financing options in writing?"
- **They returned to your gallery or past jobs** → send proof. "Happy to send a few photos of similar work we've done nearby, if it helps you decide."
- **They reopened the exact service page** → send the next step. "Looks like the [service] is still on your mind — want us to hold a slot this week?"

The body can be three sentences. The point isn't a clever email; it's a *fitting* one, sent while the spike is hot. As we covered in [why the follow-up window wins jobs](/resources/blog/follow-up-window-wins-jobs/), the window is short, and a return visit is the cleanest sign it just reopened.

## What the research says about moving first

This is where the numbers stop being borrowed from other industries and start being about contact speed itself. [78% of homeowners hire the contractor who responds first](https://pipelineon.com/blog/how-much-does-thumbtack-charge-per-lead/) — not the cheapest, not the highest-rated, the fastest. And classic lead-response research found that reaching a fresh lead within five minutes makes you up to [21× more likely to qualify it](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf) than waiting past thirty. That 21× is about how fast you reach a lead, not a promise of jobs booked — but it tells you exactly where the spike's value lives: in the first few minutes.

As for the email itself, cross-industry data shows recovery emails earn around a [45% open rate](https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/abandoned-cart-benchmarks) and that email follow-up recovers roughly [20% of abandoned visits](https://www.convertcart.com/blog/cart-abandonment-rate-statistics). Those figures come from ecommerce, so treat them as evidence that timely follow-up works — not a guarantee of your results, which vary by trade, traffic, and how fast you move. Every figure is sourced on our [stats page](/stats/).

- **Turn on return-visit alerts** so a spike from a known, consenting visitor never goes unseen.
- **Pre-write three short emails** — one for pricing, one for proof, one for the next step — so you can match and send in minutes.
- **Route alerts into your CRM** so the spike becomes an action, not a sticky note.
- **Keep it email-first.** You're reaching people who consented, never cold-calling.
