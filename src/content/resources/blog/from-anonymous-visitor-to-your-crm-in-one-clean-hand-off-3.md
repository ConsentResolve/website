## The lead arrived. Then it waited.

A recovered lead drops into your CRM at 9:40 on a Tuesday. It's a real homeowner, consented, ready to hear from you. And then it sits — in a generic "new" bucket with forty other records — until someone between jobs opens the CRM, scrolls down, notices it, figures out what it is, and finally hits send. By then it's mid-afternoon, and the homeowner has heard from two other shops.

The lead made it into the system. What it didn't do was *start anything*. Arrival and action are two different events, and the gap between them is where good leads go quiet.

## Why "in the CRM" isn't the same as "being worked"

Getting a contact into your pipeline feels like the finish line, but it's really just the starting gun — and a starting gun nobody hears doesn't start the race. If a recovered lead lands in an unsorted pile, every follow-up still depends on a human noticing it, recognizing it, and deciding to act. On a busy week, that human is on a roof.

That's the leaky bucket at the hand-off. You spent effort recovering the visitor, the lead is sitting right there, and it leaks anyway because nothing about its arrival triggered the work. The fix isn't more diligence from your crew. It's making arrival itself do the first step.

## How do you make a new lead start working on its own?

You deliver it to the right place, set up to fire the right action. That's what consent-first [CRM delivery](/resources/glossary/#crm-delivery) is built for. Rather than dumping the lead into a generic inbox, [Consent Resolve](/features/crm-delivery/) hands it into the [Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, HubSpot, Klaviyo, or GoHighLevel](/features/crm-delivery/) pipeline you already run — and lands it at the stage you've designated for a recovered web lead.

That placement is what lets your existing automations take over. If you've built a welcome email, a task assignment, or a 24-hour reminder onto that stage, it fires the moment the lead arrives. The first touch sends itself; your team picks up the conversation instead of starting it. The lead doesn't wait for a free pair of hands — the pipeline puts hands on it automatically.

## Pipeline stages and triggers that fit your day

You don't need a complicated build to make this pay. A few simple moves cover most shops:

- **Designate a stage** for recovered web leads, so they never blend into walk-ins, referrals, and old records.
- **Attach one automation to arrival** — a short, warm welcome email is plenty. "Saw you on our site — want us to put a number together?"
- **Add a task with a deadline** so a human owns the next step, with the clock already running.
- **Let scoring decide the lane.** Hot leads can route to a fast track; quieter ones into nurture. The logic behind that split is in [sorting hot, warm, and just-browsing leads](/resources/blog/hot-warm-or-just-browsing-sorting-leads-so-you-work-the-right-ones-first/), and it all rests on clean inputs — which is the whole point of [your CRM being only as good as what you feed it](/resources/blog/crm-only-as-good-as-inputs/).

Set up once, this runs on every lead after, with no daily babysitting.

## Why firing on arrival actually books jobs

The reason to automate the first touch is the same reason speed matters everywhere in follow-up. [78% of homeowners hire the contractor who responds first](https://www.vendasta.com/blog/lead-response-time/) — not the cheapest, not the highest-rated, the fastest. And classic lead-response research found that reaching a fresh lead within five minutes makes you up to [21× more likely to qualify it](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf) than waiting past thirty. That 21× is about contact speed, not a promise of jobs booked — but it explains why a lead that triggers its own first email beats one that waits for someone to notice it. Both figures are sourced on our [stats page](/stats/).

When arrival fires the first touch, you hit that window without anyone having to be at a desk.

## Set it up this week

- **Pick the pipeline stage** recovered leads should land in, in the CRM you already run.
- **Build one arrival automation** — a welcome email and a timed task is enough.
- **Test it with a live lead** so you know the trigger fires before you depend on it.
- **Keep it email-grade.** The automated first touch is email into the funnel you already run, never a cold call.

You don't need a bigger team to respond faster — you need arrival to do the first step for you.
