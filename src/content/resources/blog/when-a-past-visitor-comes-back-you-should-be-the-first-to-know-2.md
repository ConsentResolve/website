## The signal you're probably missing

Picture a homeowner who visited your site last Tuesday, looked at your roofing gallery, and left. Today they're back — same person, second time, this time on the financing page. That return is one of the loudest things a prospect can do without picking up the phone. And in most shops, nobody knows it happened.

You found out about the first visit only if they filled out a form. You found out about the second visit not at all. Meanwhile, the homeowner is sitting there comparing you against two other names, and the clock is running.

## Why the second touch is the one that converts

Plenty of advice tells you to "follow up." Almost none of it tells you *when*. That's the gap. A follow-up sent on your schedule — Monday morning batch, end-of-week catch-up — lands whenever it lands. A follow-up sent because the homeowner *just came back to your site* lands when they're already thinking about the job.

It's the difference between knocking on a door at random and knocking right after you saw the lights come on. The message can be identical; the timing changes everything. As we covered in [why the follow-up window wins jobs](/resources/blog/follow-up-window-wins-jobs/), the window is narrow — and a return visit is the cleanest sign the window just reopened.

Think about why a homeowner comes back at all. They didn't return by accident. Somewhere between the first visit and the second, they thought about the project again, maybe talked it over at the kitchen table, maybe got a number from another shop, and decided you were worth a second look. That's not idle traffic — that's a person actively working toward a decision, and circling back to your name in the middle of it. Reaching out right then doesn't feel pushy to them. It feels like good timing.

## How do you know when a past visitor comes back?

This is what a return-visit alert is for. With consent-first [multi-channel follow-up](/features/multi-channel-follow-up/), a visitor who accepted your consent banner is a known, opted-in contact. When that same person returns, you get told — not days later in a report, but as it's happening, tied to a timestamped record of their consent.

That changes the second touch from a guess to a response. Instead of "it's been a few days, I'll email everyone who visited this week," it's "this homeowner is back on the page right now — send the email that helps them finish." Some shops only see the pattern after the fact, like the buyer who took [three visits before they called](/resources/blog/three-visits-before-they-called/). An alert lets you act on visit two instead of waiting for the call that may never come.

And the second touch can be smarter, not just faster. If the alert tells you they returned to the financing page, your email can speak to financing. If they came back to your photo gallery, you can offer to show them similar jobs you've done nearby. You're not blasting a generic "just checking in" — you're answering the question they came back to ask. That's the whole advantage of knowing the return happened: it tells you not only *when* to reach out, but roughly *what about*.

## Why timing beats everything else

Once you know they're back, speed is the whole game. [78% of homeowners hire the contractor who responds first](https://www.vendasta.com/blog/lead-response-time/) — not the cheapest, not the most reviewed, the fastest. And the response window is brutally short: contacting a lead within five minutes makes you about [21× more likely to qualify them](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf) than waiting until after thirty.

The second touch also tends to land better as email. Cross-industry data shows recovery emails earn around a [45% open rate](https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/abandoned-cart-benchmarks) and that email follow-up recovers roughly [20% of abandoned visits](https://www.convertcart.com/blog/cart-abandonment-rate-statistics). Those figures come from ecommerce, so read them as evidence that timely follow-up works — not a guarantee of your numbers. Results vary by trade, traffic, and how fast you move. Every figure is sourced on our [stats page](/stats/).

## What to put in place

- **Turn on return-visit alerts.** You can't time a second touch you never knew you needed. The alert is the trigger for the whole play.
- **Have the second email pre-written.** When the alert fires, you want to send in minutes, not write from scratch. Keep it short: "Saw you were back — want us to finish that estimate?"
- **Build a fast-response habit.** The first responder usually wins; make checking and replying part of your morning and end-of-day rhythm.
- **Keep it email-grade.** Follow-up is email and retargeting into the funnel you already run — never a cold call. Leads are a flat $7, exclusive to you, never resold.
