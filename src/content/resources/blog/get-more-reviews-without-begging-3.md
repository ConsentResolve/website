## Five-star work, three-star review count

You do clean, careful work. The customers are happy when you pack up. And yet your review count limps along behind shops that do worse work but somehow have triple your stars. It's maddening, and it makes you feel like you're doing something wrong.

You're not. The work is fine. The *timing* is broken. Most contractors ask for reviews the way they ask for a favor — late, randomly, and only when they remember. That's the whole problem.

## Why the after-the-fact ask fails

Think about when the typical review request goes out. Three days later, by text, when the job is already a fading memory and the customer is back to their normal life. By then they have to stop, recall which contractor you were, find the link, log in, and write something. Every one of those steps loses people.

So you ask, get silence, feel like you're nagging, and stop asking. The reviews you earned with good work never get written — not because the customer was unhappy, but because you reached them after the moment passed.

There's a quieter cost too. Every day you wait, the odds tilt toward a different kind of review entirely. The happy customers move on with their lives and forget; the rare unhappy one is the one motivated enough to go hunting for the review page on their own. Ask late and you let the squeaky wheels self-select. Ask at the peak, and you capture the broad middle of genuinely satisfied people who'd happily leave five stars if you just made it easy in the moment. Timing doesn't only raise your review count — it raises your average rating.

## When is the right moment to ask for a review?

Right at the close-out. The work is done, it looks good, the customer can see it with their own eyes, and the goodwill is at its peak. That's the window. A homeowner standing in front of a clean install or a finished repair, genuinely pleased, is as likely to leave you five stars as they will ever be. Wait, and that feeling cools by the hour.

The trick isn't asking harder. It's asking *earlier* — and making it a fixed step in how you finish every job, so it happens whether or not anyone remembers to.

## What "systematic" actually looks like

Build it into the close-out the same way you build in the final walkthrough. Same sentence, every time, said while you're wrapping up: *"If you were happy with how this turned out, a quick review honestly makes a huge difference for a shop our size."* It's true, it's warm, and because you say it every single time, it never feels like a special request.

Then remove every bit of friction. Pull up the review link on your own phone and hand it to them, or text it before you leave the driveway while you're still standing there. Don't make them hunt. The shorter the path between "yes, I'll do that" and the keyboard, the more reviews actually get written.

Most field [CRM](/resources/glossary/#crm) tools — Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan — can fire a review link automatically the second you mark a job complete. That's the close-out ask, systematized: it goes out at the peak moment, every job, without you lifting a finger. The verbal ask plants the seed while you're standing there; the automated text gives them the link before the goodwill cools. Together they catch both the person who'll do it on the spot and the one who needs it in their pocket for later that evening.

The point of building it into the tool is that it survives a busy week. When you're slammed, the review ask is the first thing that falls off your plate — and a busy stretch is exactly when you're finishing the most jobs and earning the most goodwill. Automate it once and your busiest weeks become your best review weeks instead of your worst.

## Follow up once — then let it go

Some people mean to and forget. One gentle nudge the next day catches most of them: *"No pressure at all — here's that link again if you have a minute."* One follow-up. Not three, not a guilt trip. A single reminder respects the customer and still recovers the people who simply got busy. Begging is asking five times; a system asks at the right time and follows up once.

## Put it on autopilot this week

- **Write the close-out line** and say it on every job, not just the ones that went especially well.
- **Hand over the link in person** or text it before you leave — never make the customer go looking.
- **Automate the trigger** so a review request fires when you mark the job done, with one polite follow-up the next day.
- **Stop chasing old jobs.** Focus the energy on nailing the moment of completion going forward.
