## There's someone on your site right now

Open your analytics and you'll usually see it: somebody is on your site at this very moment. Maybe they're on your pricing page. Maybe they've been reading reviews for two minutes. They're weighing whether to call you.

Now close the tab and try to remember who they were. You can't — because the dashboard told you *someone* was there, not *who*, and by the time you read tomorrow's report they're long gone. That gap between "a visit happened" and "I can do something about it" is where most home-service leads quietly leak out.

## Why a report you read later is already too late

The clock is brutal. The average visitor spends about [87 seconds](https://spectruminfinite.com/blogs/average-time-spent-on-website-2025/) on a website before moving on, and across home-service sites roughly [98% of visitors never convert or identify themselves](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/conversion-rate-benchmarks). They price the job, they compare you to two other shops, and they leave — usually before you've finished your coffee.

A weekly traffic report can't fix that, because it tells you about interest that already cooled. By Thursday, the homeowner who was hot on Monday has either booked someone else or shelved the project. Seeing the visit *after* it mattered is just a record of a lead you missed.

## What does it look like to see who's on your site right now?

This is what real-time [visitor identification](/resources/glossary/#visitor-identification) is for. When a visitor accepts a clear consent banner, [Consent Resolve](/features/visitor-identification/) turns that anonymous-but-consenting visitor into a named, email-grade contact as it happens — a live "who's here now" view rather than a report you dig up days later. No form fill, no phone number to cold-call. Just a real contact, while the interest is still warm.

The shift is from *counting* visits to *acting* on them. Instead of "we had 400 visitors last week," you get "this consented homeowner just spent four minutes on the AC replacement page — here's their email." One of those sentences is a statistic. The other is a job you can go book.

## Why real-time beats waiting for a form

Forms are the slow lane. Static opt-in forms pull in only about [2% of organic email-list growth](https://www.retainful.com/blog/grow-your-email-list) a month, because they ask the visitor to stop, decide, and type — exactly when they're least willing to. Most people never will.

Automated, consent-first capture works the other way around. In cross-industry data, automated capture brings in [10–15× more subscribers than forms alone](https://bdow.com/stories/email-signup-benchmarks/) — that's ecommerce-derived evidence, and your results will vary by trade, traffic, and how you follow up, but the direction is plain: catching consented visitors as they browse beats waiting for the rare one who fills out a form. The numbers behind all of this live on our [stats page](/stats/), each one sourced.

## What to do with a real-time view this week

- **Watch the high-intent pages live.** When a consented visitor lands on your pricing, financing, or service pages, that's your signal — they're shopping, not idly browsing.
- **Send one helpful email the same day.** Short and friendly: "Saw you were looking at AC replacement — want us to finish that quote?" Same-day beats next-week every time.
- **Route the contact into your funnel.** Drop it into the email tool you already run — HubSpot, Klaviyo, GoHighLevel — so follow-up is automatic, not a sticky note.
- **Stop reading the rear-view mirror.** A weekly report tells you who you lost. A real-time view lets you keep them.

You're already paying to put people on your site. The only question is whether you find out who they are while it still matters — or read about them after they've booked someone else. Seeing your consented visitors in real time, with no form in the way, is how a busy storefront finally gets to greet the people walking in.
