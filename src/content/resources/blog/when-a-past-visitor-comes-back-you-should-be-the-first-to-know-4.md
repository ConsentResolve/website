## The season the old projects come back

Spring cleaning isn't just closets. Homeowners walk the yard, look up at the gutters, notice the fence leaning, and remember the project they meant to handle last fall. The list they shelved in November is suddenly back on the fridge.

And here's what they do next: they go back to the contractor websites they bookmarked months ago. The same homeowner who priced you in October and went quiet is quietly back on your site in March — pricing the exact job again. The only question is whether you notice before they move on to the next shop on their list.

## Why a return visit slips past most shops

The trouble is that a second visit looks like nothing in your analytics. It's one more number in a daily count, indistinguishable from a stranger who'll never call. Nobody on your team is sitting there watching traffic, and even if they were, they couldn't tell that *this* visitor is the one who almost booked you in the fall.

So the warmest lead you'll get all spring — a homeowner who already considered you and is now reconsidering — arrives invisibly and leaves the same way. You didn't lose them on price or on reviews. You lost them because you never knew they came back.

## How do you know the moment a past visitor returns?

This is the job of [multi-channel follow-up](/resources/glossary/#multi-channel-follow-up) built on consent-first identification. When a consented visitor returns to your site, [Consent Resolve](/features/multi-channel-follow-up/) flags it and feeds your follow-up automatically — you find out a known contact is back *while they're still browsing*, not in next week's report. No watching dashboards, no guessing.

That head start is the whole advantage, because speed decides these. [78% of homeowners hire the contractor who responds first](https://pipelineon.com/blog/how-much-does-thumbtack-charge-per-lead/), and reaching out within five minutes gives you [21× better odds of qualifying the lead](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf) than waiting half an hour. A returning visitor is already leaning your way; being first to greet them is how you close the gap.

## Why following up the second visit pays off

Re-engagement isn't a long shot — it's where a lot of the recoverable business lives. In cross-industry data, email follow-up recovers about [20% of would-be-lost visitors](https://www.convertcart.com/blog/cart-abandonment-rate-statistics), and recovery emails see roughly a [45% open rate](https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/abandoned-cart-benchmarks) — people open the message when it's relevant to something they were just thinking about. Those figures are ecommerce-derived, so treat them as evidence that timely follow-up works, not a guaranteed contractor result; yours will vary by trade, traffic, and follow-up. Every figure is sourced on our [stats page](/stats/).

A homeowner revisiting a spring project is about as relevant an audience as you'll find. The email that lands the day they come back doesn't feel like marketing — it feels like good timing.

## What to do this spring

- **Treat a return visit as a buying signal.** When a consented past visitor comes back, that's a project moving from "someday" to "soon."
- **Be the first to respond.** Let the return trigger an immediate, friendly email — "saw you back on the site, ready to dust off that quote?" — so you beat the slower shops.
- **Run it across channels.** Pair the email with retargeting so a returning homeowner sees you in more than one place, into the funnel you already run.
- **Keep it in your CRM.** Route returning-visitor follow-up through the tool you already use — Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, HubSpot — so nothing falls through.

Spring cleaning will send last fall's browsers back to your door whether you're watching or not. The shops that win the season aren't the ones with the most new traffic — they're the ones who notice the old visitor the moment they return and say hello first.
