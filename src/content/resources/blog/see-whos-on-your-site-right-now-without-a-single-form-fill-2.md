## Watch one visit happen

Forget the pitch for a second. Let's just watch one homeowner move through your site, minute by minute, and mark exactly where the lead is won or lost.

It's a Tuesday night. A homeowner whose water heater is making a noise pulls up your site on their phone. They're not ready to call — they're in the *figuring-it-out* stage. On most sites, what happens next is nothing: they look, they leave, they're gone. This field guide is about the version where they don't disappear.

## Minute one: the storefront with no doorbell

Your visitor arrives. Right now, on a typical setup, your site has no idea who just walked in. It's a busy storefront with the lights on and nobody at the counter. The visitor browses for a bit — the average is about [87 seconds](https://spectruminfinite.com/blogs/average-time-spent-on-website-2025/) before attention drifts — and across home-service sites, roughly [98% of visitors never convert or identify themselves](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/conversion-rate-benchmarks).

That's the moment the lead leaks. Not because the traffic was bad — it showed up, it was interested — but because there was no way to know who it was. This is the gap [visitor identification](/features/visitor-identification/) is built to close, and the rest of the walkthrough is how.

## Minute two: the consent moment

As the homeowner settles in, they see a clear consent banner. This is the hinge of the whole sequence. If they accept, two things become true at once: you're now allowed to identify them, and that agreement is logged with a timestamp. If they don't accept, nothing happens — and that's by design. [Visitor identification](/resources/glossary/#visitor-identification) only ever works on the consenting.

I want to be plain here because it matters: this isn't sneaking. It's the opposite. The banner is the moment the visitor opts in, and it's what makes everything downstream lawful instead of risky.

## Minutes three to five: the browse

Now the consented homeowner does what shoppers do. They open your tankless-water-heater page. They check your reviews. Maybe they glance at financing. They still don't fill out a form — and here's the field-guide point: *they were never going to.* Forms are a tiny net. Traditional opt-in forms grow an email list by only about [2% a month](https://www.retainful.com/blog/grow-your-email-list); they catch the rare visitor who's ready to commit and miss everyone still deciding. This homeowner is deciding. A form would've caught nothing.

## How does the visitor become a lead?

Here's the step most people are fuzzy on. The consented visit is matched to a real, email-grade contact and delivered to you as a lead — a name and an email — without the homeowner ever touching a form. The browse-and-leave that would have been a dead end is now an entry in your follow-up.

And this is where the scale shows up. Automated capture like this is documented, cross-industry, to bring in [10–15 times](https://bdow.com/stories/email-signup-benchmarks/) more subscribers than forms alone. That figure comes from ecommerce, so treat it as evidence that the approach works rather than a number your shop is guaranteed to hit — results vary by trade, traffic, and follow-up. But the mechanism is the same everywhere: stop relying on the tiny form net, and you keep far more of the people who were already there. The sourcing for all of this lives on our [stats page](/stats/).

## Minute six and after: the follow-up

The visit ended, but the relationship didn't. Wednesday morning, the homeowner who browsed water heaters gets one helpful email — not a cold call, never a cold call — into the funnel you already run. That's the whole arc: arrive, consent, browse, get captured, get followed up with. Five steps, and the only one most sites have today is the first.

## Run the field guide on your own site

- **Map your own leak.** Pull up your visitor count versus your form fills. The gap between them is the lead pool you're not catching yet.
- **Put the consent banner where it's seen.** It's the hinge — clear, plain, early. No consent, no lead.
- **Have the follow-up ready before you flip it on.** A short, friendly email queued up means recovered visitors hear from you the same day.
- **Keep it consent-first and email-grade.** Flat $7 per lead, exclusive, never resold — and you reach people who agreed to hear from you.

Once you've watched the steps, the mystery's gone. It's just a storefront that finally knows who walked in.
