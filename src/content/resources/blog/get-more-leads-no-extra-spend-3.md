## You already paid for the leads

Every contractor I talk to wants the same thing: more leads. And almost every one of them reaches for the same lever first — spend more on ads. Pour more into Google, try a new platform, boost the budget.

Here's what gets missed. You've already paid to get a stream of homeowners onto your site this month. The vast majority of them left without leaving a name. The leads you're chasing with a bigger budget are, in large part, people who were *already on your site* — and walked.

## Where the budget actually leaks

Across home-service websites, roughly [98% of visitors never convert or identify themselves](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/conversion-rate-benchmarks). You can read that as a conversion stat, or you can read it the way it actually hits your wallet: for every hundred clicks you paid for, ninety-eight left anonymous.

That's not a traffic problem. The traffic showed up — you bought it. It's a leak. And the instinct to fix a leak by pouring more water into the bucket is exactly backwards. More ad spend just sends more people through the same hole.

## What if the cheapest leads are the ones already on your site?

This is the reframe that changes the math. The most expensive lead you'll ever get is a brand-new click from a cold homeowner. The cheapest is the visitor who's *already here* — you've paid the acquisition cost, they've shown intent, and all that's left is to keep them from vanishing.

That's what [consent-first visitor identification](/resources/glossary/#visitor-identification) does. When a homeowner accepts a clear consent banner, [Consent Resolve](/features/visitor-identification/) turns that consenting visitor into a real contact — a name and a consented email, logged with a timestamp — with no form to fill. There's no phone number to cold-call; follow-up is email, into the funnel you already run. You're not buying new traffic. You're plugging the leak in the traffic you've got.

## The evidence: recovery beats acquisition

Identifying visitors and acting on that data has been documented at a [6–10× return](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/how-brands-boost-roi-with-smart-data) across industries. That figure is ecommerce-derived, so treat it as cross-industry evidence that recovery works rather than a promise for your shop — results vary by trade, traffic, and follow-up.

But the principle is hard to argue with: dollars spent keeping the visitors you already attracted go further than dollars spent attracting brand-new ones. If you want to see how the channels stack up on cost, our [comparison hub](/resources/compare/) lays it out, and every figure here is sourced on our [stats page](/stats/).

## Why this beats buying leads from a platform

There's a second way contractors try to get "more leads without more work" — buying them from a lead-seller platform. On the surface it looks like the same idea: pay for a lead, get a job. But it isn't. Those leads are usually shared with several other pros, sold at a premium, and the homeowner gets quoted by everyone at once. You're not buying a customer; you're buying a spot in a five-way race for one.

Recovering your own visitors is the opposite of that. The homeowner was on *your* site, looking at *your* work — they already have a reason to lean your way. The lead is exclusive to you, never resold, and it cost a fraction of a shared one. So when I say "more leads without more spend," I don't mean adding another lead bill. I mean turning the traffic you've already bought into contacts that are yours alone.

## How do I know recovery is actually paying off?

The honest way to measure this is simple, and it's the same way you'd measure any marketing dollar: track the recovered leads, track how many become quotes, and track how many become jobs. Because each recovered lead is a flat **$7** and exclusive to you, the math tends to clear quickly — even a modest close rate on a contact you'd otherwise have lost entirely is found money.

A couple of practical notes so the measurement is clean. Give recovered leads the same fast, helpful follow-up you'd give a phone call — a contact you ignore is a contact wasted, no matter how cheap it was. And keep it consent-first: every visitor you reach has accepted a clear banner and carries a timestamped consent record, so your follow-up is email-grade and welcome, not a cold interruption. Do that, and you'll see the same pattern most shops do — more booked work from the exact same number of clicks you were already paying for.

## What to do this week

- **Stop the leak before you open the tap.** Turn on consent-first identification so the traffic you already pay for stops leaving anonymous.
- **Don't touch the ad budget yet.** Prove out recovery on your current spend first — that's the whole point.
- **Have one email ready.** A short, friendly nudge — "want us to put a quote together?" — sent the same day a visitor browsed.
- **Respond first.** The shop that reaches a ready buyer fastest usually books the job.

You don't need a bigger budget to get more leads this month. You need to stop letting the traffic you already bought walk out the door. Each recovered lead is flat **$7**, exclusive to you, never resold — and it came from people you were already paying to reach.
