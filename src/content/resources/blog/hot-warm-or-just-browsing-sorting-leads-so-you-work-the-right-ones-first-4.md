## The morning your lead list doubled overnight

Spring does this every year. The first warm stretch lands, homeowners start lining up projects they put off all winter, and the leads pile in faster than you can work them. You open your list Monday and there are twice as many names as last week — and somewhere in that pile are three or four people ready to book today.

The trouble is they don't stand out. On a flat list, the homeowner who priced your service page twice this weekend sits right next to the one who skimmed your blog once and bounced. Same row, same look, no order.

## Why a flat list quietly costs you the busy weeks

When volume is low, working leads top-to-bottom is fine — you get to everyone. But the spring spike breaks that. Now there are more leads than follow-up hours, and the order you work them in starts deciding which jobs you win.

If your hottest lead is sitting at row 28 because it came in late Sunday night, it waits while you grind through warmer-than-cold browsers up top. By the time you reach it, the homeowner has called someone else. The lead wasn't lost because you ignored it. It was lost because nothing told you to work it first.

## How do you know which leads to work first when they all just came in?

This is what [lead scoring](/resources/glossary/#lead-scoring) is for. Every recovered, consented visitor arrives carrying signals about how ready they are — and [Consent Resolve](/features/lead-scoring/) reads them and turns them into a score.

A homeowner who viewed your pricing and service pages, came back twice over the weekend, and was on the site an hour ago scores hot. Someone who landed once on a blog post three weeks ago scores cold. You're not guessing from a name in a field anymore; you're reading intent that was already in the visit.

So when Monday's doubled list loads, it loads in order. The ready buyers are at the top. You work the first hour of the busiest week on the jobs most likely to close — same leads, smarter sequence.

## Why working the hot ones first wins more jobs

Order matters because speed matters. [78% of homeowners hire the contractor who responds first](https://www.vendasta.com/blog/lead-response-time/) — not the cheapest, not the highest-rated, the fastest. And contacting a lead within five minutes gives you [21× better odds of qualifying it](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf) than waiting past thirty.

Those advantages do the most good when they land on a ready buyer. Reaching a cold browser in five minutes doesn't help much — they weren't going to book today anyway. Reaching a hot lead first is where the season's jobs get won. Scoring makes sure your fastest follow-up goes there. These benchmarks behave differently by trade, traffic, and how you follow up, but the pattern is steady: first contact, on the right lead, books work. The figures behind it are on our [stats page](/stats/).

## How to run a spike week by score

- **Sort by score before you touch anything.** Don't read your list in arrival order during the rush — let the hot leads float to the top first.
- **Spend your morning on hot.** The first follow-up hours of a busy day are your most valuable; aim them at the ready buyers, not the browsers.
- **Put warm and cold on a slower track.** A short email nudge or a [retargeting](/features/instant-retarget/) pass keeps them in your funnel without eating the hours your hot leads need.
- **Recheck the list midday.** New leads keep landing during the spring rush; a fresh sort catches the ones that came in hot since morning.

The spring spike isn't a problem of too few leads — it's a problem of order. Get the order right, and a doubled list becomes a fuller schedule instead of a missed pile.
