## The Simple Version

**Consent-first means you only contact people who agreed to hear from you, on the channel they agreed to.** That is the whole idea. If someone gave you their email and said "email me," you email them. You do not text them, and you do not call. Permission is specific, and you stay inside it.

## Why This Confuses People

The phrase sounds like a privacy-policy footnote, so contractors assume it is legal busywork that slows marketing down. It is the opposite. The confusion comes from years of marketing tools that treated any contact detail as fair game — buy a list, blast it, see what sticks. Laws like the TCPA, CCPA, and CIPA have made that approach expensive and risky. "Consent-first" is just the clean way to do the same job without the liability.

## Real-World Example

Two roofers each get a homeowner's information after a website visit. The first roofer scraped a phone number from a data tool and cold-calls during dinner; the homeowner never agreed to that, and the roofer is now one complaint away from a costly problem. The second roofer received a name and an email the homeowner *consented* to share, and sends one helpful follow-up: "Thanks for stopping by — want us to finish your quote, or give us a call?" Same goal, but only one of them built something they can keep doing.

## What It Means For Your Business

Consent-first is not a brake on growth; it is what makes growth safe to repeat. A lead tagged with the consent it gave you can be dropped straight into the right follow-up — email for an email opt-in, a call or text only when the person agreed to that. You keep a record of what each person allowed, you honor "stop" the moment you hear it, and you never have to wonder whether a campaign is going to backfire. That is the difference between a busy pipeline and a defensible one.

## What To Do Next

Capture consent clearly at the point someone becomes a lead, and track the *level* of consent — email-grade versus phone-grade — so your follow-up never outruns it.
